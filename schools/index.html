The problem you already know

You already know what's broken.

You've seen it from inside the building. Perkins V requires documented career readiness indicators — and most districts are producing them manually, if at all. The college-default culture that steers students away from high-demand technical fields. The seniors who arrive at decision-day without a framework for the decision. And the moment when a board or state agency asks for evidence and "we ran some programs" is not a sufficient answer.

The honest gap is not effort. It is between what your accountability obligations require and what your current tools actually produce.

Tuesday was designed for this environment — not invented in spite of it.

01
Accountability pressure. Perkins V mandates specific career readiness indicators. Most districts are still reporting from guidance notes, printed worksheets, and survey data that doesn't hold up to scrutiny.
02
Perkins compliance risk. The indicators require documented evidence of student career exploration activity — not a sign-in sheet from a college fair. That evidence is what's missing, and auditors have noticed.
03
College-default liability. Pushing every student toward four-year college when your local labor market needs skilled trades and technical workers isn't just unhelpful — it's increasingly a reputational and funding exposure.
04
Accountability gap. Perkins V funding and state career readiness mandates require documented evidence of student pathway engagement — not participation counts. "We did career exploration" doesn't satisfy that bar anymore.
